# Assurance Scope

FTQC Assurance provides a bounded evidence-to-decision layer for fault-tolerant quantum computing programs whose architecture, experiments, estimates, interfaces, and expert judgments change over time.

## Profile-owned controls

The profile provides machine-checkable structure for:

- FTQC system-concept identity and architecture revision;
- resource-estimation assumptions, result identity, and review state;
- evidence classes and explicit applicability conditions;
- applicability basis and declared-versus-established authority state;
- internal, external, and independent-review class separation;
- expert review scope, evidence reviewed, exclusions, uncertainty, and reopen triggers;
- change propagation through declared FMA dependencies;
- decision-gating conditions and explicit decision reopening;
- source-neutral synthetic reference evaluation.

## Governed responsibilities

The systems, experts, laboratories, programs, and authorities responsible for the actual system retain ownership of:

- quantum-physics validity and interpretation;
- QEC theory, decoder validity, thresholds, and mathematical closure;
- resource-estimator correctness and configuration;
- hardware fidelity, error and loss characterization, calibration, and metrology;
- experimental truth and reproducibility;
- supplier and manufacturing qualification;
- safety, security, contracting, legal, regulatory, and government-program applicability;
- final mission, program, investment, acquisition, or deployment decisions.

FMA-Q can establish whether declared artifacts remain structurally connected to a decision basis. It cannot convert declared inputs into scientific truth.

## Public release policy

Public examples are synthetic and source-neutral. Mission-specific or protected evidence remains in governed systems. The public profile must not contain real customer, partner, supplier, investor, operator, program, or proprietary architecture identity.
