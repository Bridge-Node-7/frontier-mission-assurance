# Independence and Conflict Policy

Bridge Node 7 treats independence as an assurance asset.

This policy defines the minimum conflict screen required before Frontier Mission Assurance work is accepted where the same organization, system, evidence, or technical decision could later be subject to independent review.

## Principle

> **Independence is capital. Do not spend it accidentally.**

Commercial work for a performer does not automatically make later independent review impossible. The risk is scope-specific and depends on what Bridge Node 7 designed, advocated, accessed, decided, or was paid to influence.

The correct control is a documented screen before consequential work begins.

## Required pre-engagement screen

Record, at minimum:

1. **Client and system identity** — who the work concerns and which bounded system, subsystem, claim set, or decision is in scope.
2. **Role class** — advisory, evidence-architecture, implementation, design participation, advocacy, external domain review, or explicitly governed independent V&V.
3. **Proprietary access** — whether nonpublic architecture, source data, supplier, partner, experiment, model, schedule, or program information will be accessed.
4. **Design participation** — whether Bridge Node 7 will choose, optimize, author, approve, or materially shape the design later subject to review.
5. **Decision participation** — whether Bridge Node 7 will own, approve, or advocate for the consequential decision.
6. **Future self-review risk** — whether later work could require Bridge Node 7 to independently assess its own prior analysis, implementation, recommendation, or evidence selection.
7. **Financial dependence** — whether commercial concentration or compensation structure could reasonably impair perceived objectivity.
8. **Government conflict considerations** — whether an applicable solicitation, contract, organizational-conflict rule, disclosure requirement, or evaluator-independence condition may apply.
9. **Mitigation** — scope partitioning, information barriers, recusal, separate personnel, third-party review, disclosure, or refusal.
10. **Disposition** — ACCEPT, ACCEPT WITH CONDITIONS, HOLD FOR COUNSEL / CONTRACTING REVIEW, or DECLINE.

## Role boundaries

### Builder-side verification readiness

Allowed when clearly bounded.

Bridge Node 7 may help a technical organization make its claims, assumptions, evidence, dependencies, expert gates, and decision basis more reviewable without representing the work as independent validation.

### External domain review

Allowed only when the reviewer, evidence basis, exclusions, and relationship to the client are explicit.

External review is not automatically independent V&V.

### Independent V&V

Use the label only when independence is explicitly governed by the applicable process, contract, solicitation, authority, and technical-review structure.

Do not upgrade an advisory or commercial review into independent V&V by terminology.

## Red flags requiring escalation

Escalate before accepting or continuing work when any of the following is true:

- Bridge Node 7 would later be asked to independently validate a design it materially helped create.
- The engagement requires advocacy for a technical claim that Bridge Node 7 may later need to challenge.
- Compensation depends on a favorable technical conclusion.
- The same personnel would author and independently validate the same critical evidence or result.
- A government opportunity introduces organizational-conflict, disclosure, or independence requirements.
- The customer requests language implying certification, government readiness, or independent validation beyond the governed scope.

## Public repository boundary

Real conflict screens, client identities, proprietary facts, legal analyses, contracting advice, and government-specific determinations belong in governed private workspaces.

The public repository documents the process only.

## Authority

This policy is an internal assurance control, not legal or contracting advice. Government-contracting counsel or the relevant contracting authority should review real organizational-conflict questions when required.
